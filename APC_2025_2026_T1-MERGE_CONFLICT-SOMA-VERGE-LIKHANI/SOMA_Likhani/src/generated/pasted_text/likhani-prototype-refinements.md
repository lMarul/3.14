1. Terms of Use page — fix spacing and breathing room

Refine the Terms of Use page so it feels more spacious and readable. Right now the Back button and the page title are too close together. Increase the vertical spacing between the Back button and the “Terms of Use” heading so there is a clearer hierarchy and more breathing room. The Back button should sit at the top-left aligned to the same content container as the page body. Use the same left page margin as the main text column. Recommended spacing: 32px from the top of the content area to the Back button, then 32px to 40px between the Back button and the page title, then 16px between the title and the “Last updated” line, then 40px before the main body text begins. Also make sure the text column width is readable and not too wide, with comfortable paragraph spacing and consistent spacing between numbered sections. The page should feel like a formal document page, not cramped.

2. Hero section arrow buttons — stop overlapping the title

Fix the left and right hero navigation arrows so they never overlap the title, subtitle, or any hero text when hovered or in normal state. The arrows should be positioned relative to the hero image area only, not the text block. Anchor the arrows vertically to the center of the visible hero image region, not to the full hero container height if the text block is separate. Keep them inset from the left and right edges of the hero image by around 24px–32px. Ensure their hover state does not enlarge or cast shadows in a way that visually collides with the title. The arrows must always remain readable and accessible, but they should not compete with the hero copy. Preserve the cinematic feel, but make the controls feel secondary to the featured work.

3. Featured section — add short appreciation / context instead of just a direct CTA

Refine the featured section so it feels more like a curated showcase rather than just a banner with a “View Work” button. Keep the CTA, but add a short descriptive and appreciative text block that gives context to the work and highlights the creators. Below or beside the title, include a short 2–3 sentence editorial description that introduces the featured project in a calm, academic, cinematic tone. Then add a compact metadata/appreciation block that can include: Director, Production, and Cast. This should not feel like film credits pasted in full — keep it short, elegant, and clearly structured. For example: “Directed by… Produced by… Cast…” in a subtle, organized text stack. The goal is to help users appreciate the work and its creators before clicking, so the homepage feels curated and reflective, not just click-driven. Keep this section visually balanced and not too dense.
